Logo: a crow in flight clutching a tarnished silver coin
Colors: purple and silver, members are given a long vented dark purple cloak trimmed with black feathers
Special language: Crow scratch and thieves cant

Traits:
- Typically dislikes mages, as mainly the rich can afford to learn wizardry
- They have a very strong moral compass and will often act on gut feelings
- Impatience in the justice system

The Brotherhood of Crows is a clandestine organization of thieves, spies, and assassins who operate with a strict code of honor and a unique sense of justice. They see themselves as a necessary counterbalance to corruption and tyranny, often targeting the wealthy elite, corrupt officials, and oppressive regimes. While they are criminals in the eyes of the law, they are often seen as heroes by the common folk, who benefit from their acts of redistribution and their efforts to expose injustice.

They have many nests across Yorin, but their main roost is Lyndon. The hierarchy within the brotherhood is based off merit and skill. They usually act alone off information present during their meetings. Many have a bounty board and take requests from the common folk. In rare cases Crows will seek out extra muscle from adventurers and the like. Its not uncommon for an adventurer to become a part of the brotherhood. Many crows see and value the struggle of poverty as a rite of passage within their ranks. Many use it as measure to show how tough they are. Oddly enough there are a few familial lineages that are the roots of the Crows foot holds in various cities.

Each mission is vetted by the nest's **Murder**, a smaller group of experienced crows. Each murder has a leader Crow known as the nest's **Plenty**, their rule is final. Named after the founder of the Crows **Chief Plenty**. The Murder members makes suggestions while the Plenty makes decisions; not all mission approvals get to the Plenty just the high priority ones.

This structure can lead to some disagreements and bad blood, younger newer Crows typically are against the hierarchy. They view it as archaic, but there is wisdom in letting elders lead.

Not all commoners respect the crows; many feel its unjust they go unpunished for their crimes.
